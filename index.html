Abstract

Differences in hand geometry, joint parameterization, and kinematic topology pose challenges for cross-embodiment dexterous grasp generation. However, spatial relations among links alone are insufficient to fully capture the hand's kinematic topology. We propose DexGraspFlow, a conditional link-level $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ flow-matching framework integrating geometric relations and kinematic topology.

The framework encodes local object geometry, link geometry, and current poses. A dual-stream DiT models link--link coordination and link--object interaction, while attention biases derived from kinematic-tree distances provide explicit structural information for modeling evolving geometric interactions. The model jointly learns translational and rotational velocity fields, integrates them to generate link poses, and recovers joint configurations through inverse kinematics. Additionally, inference-time palm-orientation guidance enables controllable grasp generation without retraining.

Across three robotic hands and 30 object--hand pairs, DexGraspFlow achieves a 95.33% average simulation success rate, with an average generation latency, including inverse kinematics, approximately 49% lower than that of a state-of-the-art baseline. Further experiments demonstrate zero-shot adaptability to seven finger-removal variants and palm-orientation control. A model trained on demonstrations for the deployed hand achieves a 91% success rate over 100 real-robot grasping trials, supporting its practical applicability.

1. Motivation

Cross-embodiment dexterous grasp synthesis faces two fundamental challenges:

(a) Kinematic Topology Awareness: Preserving Skeleton Feasibility

The Problem: Spatial Euclidean distance ignores the robot's kinematic skeleton. Denoising in unconstrained 3D space causes severe joint hyperextensions, finger crossovers, and kinematic invalidity.

Our Solution: We inject an invariant kinematic hop-count matrix $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{topo}}$ as a structural attention bias, constraining continuous flow matching to evolve strictly along the robot's physical kinematic manifold.

(b) User-Decided Controllable Generation: Meeting Downstream Task Intent

The Problem: Practical robotic manipulation requires grasp poses aligned with downstream task intents (e.g., pouring from the side, bin-picking from above). Existing cross-embodiment models rely on blind random sampling or require expensive model retraining for every new condition.

Our Solution: We formulate training-free inference-time geodesic guidance on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$. Given user-specified approach directions, closed-form rotational velocity corrections $\Delta\mathbf{u}_{t,p}^{\omega} = s(t)\lambda_c\mathbf{R}_{t,p}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\omega}_t^c$ are injected into the predicted velocity field ($\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t,p}^{\omega} = \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{t,p}^{\omega} + \Delta\mathbf{u}_{t,p}^{\omega}$) at each integration step, smoothly steering the palm toward target poses while preserving physics-compliant contacts.

2. Method Overview: Dual-Stream DiT & Geodesic Flow Matching

DexGraspFlow formulates multi-link dexterous grasp generation as conditional $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ flow matching on the rigid-body Lie group, parameterized by a geometry- and topology-aware dual-stream Transformer:

1. Kinematic & Geometric Encoding

The target object point cloud is partitioned into local patch tokens, while each robot link is discretized into surface points and encoded into static link tokens. Concatenating with dynamic rigid poses yields link-state tokens $s_{i,t}$.

2. Dual-Stream Structural Attention

Link--Link Stream: Injects topological distance biases $\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{topo}}$ derived from $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{topo}}$ to preserve skeleton feasibility.
Link--Object Stream: Injects physical-relation biases $\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{geom}}$ to accurately model contact geometry.

3. Geodesic Guidance & Batched IK

The network jointly learns translational and angular velocity fields $(v_\theta^x, v_\theta^\omega)$. Training-free geodesic guidance smoothly steers palm orientation during integration, followed by batched GPU Pyroki IK recovery.

6. Real-World Robot Deployment (91% Success Rate)

To evaluate real-world feasibility, DexGraspFlow was deployed on a physical robotic workstation consisting of a ROKAE industrial manipulator, a Linker Hand L20Lite dexterous five-finger hand, and an Intel RealSense D435i camera. The system evaluated zero-shot grasp transfer across 10 physical objects never observed during training.

100 Physical Trials Benchmark: Across 10 unseen daily objects (10 independent trials per object, total 100 trials), DexGraspFlow achieved an overall success rate of 91/100 (91.0%), firmly validating its zero-shot physical transfer and robust contact synthesis.
